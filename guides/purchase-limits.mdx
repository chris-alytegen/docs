---
title: "Ticket limits per customer"
description: "Cap how many tickets one customer can hold or buy for an event, across every agent, card and queued purchase."
---

A per-purchase maximum stops one large order. It does not stop the same customer placing many small
ones, through one agent or several. A **ticket limit per customer** does: you set it on an event,
and Alyte counts every ticket that customer holds or buys for that event, whichever agent or route
they use.

You set it **per event**, because the right number differs: a stadium might allow 6 and an
intimate gig 2. There is no shop-wide value. One API key with `merchant:write` manages it.

## Set a limit

```bash
curl -X PUT "https://app.alytegen.com/v1/integration/events/$EVENT_ID/purchase-limit" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $ALYTE_API_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "maxPerCustomer": 4, "maxHeldPerCustomer": 2 }'
```

```json
{
  "purchaseLimit": {
    "eventId": "evt_…", "merchantId": "mrc_…",
    "maxPerCustomer": 4, "maxHeldPerCustomer": 2,
    "revision": 1, "updatedAt": "2026-11-04T10:30:00.000Z"
  },
  "readiness": { "identifiers": ["account", "card"], "ticketsCounted": 8, "ticketsNotCounted": 3 }
}
```

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `maxPerCustomer` | Required, 1 to 1,000. The most tickets one customer can have for the event, held or bought. |
| `maxHeldPerCustomer` | Optional, at most `maxPerCustomer`. The most **unpaid** tickets one customer can hold at once, so nobody can tie up stock in holds they never pay for. Leave it out for no separate cap. |
| `countWaitingWatches` | Optional, `true` or `false`. Whether [queued purchases](#queued-purchases) count toward the limit while they wait. Leave it out to leave it unset, which counts; `GET` then returns `null`. |

`PUT` replaces the whole limit: sending `{ "maxPerCustomer": 4 }` alone removes an earlier
`maxHeldPerCustomer`. Values must be whole numbers and `countWaitingWatches` must be `true` or
`false`; `"4"`, `4.5`, `null`, `"false"` or an unknown field is refused with `400`, and nothing is
saved.

| You want to | Call |
|---|---|
| Read the limit | `GET /v1/integration/events/{id}/purchase-limit` (`merchant:read`). `purchaseLimit` is `null` when the event has none. |
| Remove it | `DELETE /v1/integration/events/{id}/purchase-limit`. Returns `{ "cleared": true }`, or `false` if there was none. |

Alyte records every change with the values before and after. The SDK has no methods for this yet;
call the REST routes.

## Who counts as one customer

Alyte recognises a customer by two things. Two purchases that share either one count as the same
customer.

- **The Alyte buyer account**: the buyer an agent belongs to. Two agents the same buyer created
  count as one customer. An agent buying for someone other than the buyer it belongs to cannot use
  that buyer's allowance.
- **The card**: recorded when the buyer saves it. Two buyer accounts paying with the same card count
  as one customer. Alyte keeps a keyed hash of the card's identifier, never the identifier itself,
  and never shows it to you or the agent.
  - **Stripe**: the card's fingerprint, with no setup on your side.
  - **Adyen**: the card alias, which Adyen returns only if your account is set to send it. See
    [Adyen: send the card alias](#adyen-send-the-card-alias).

Some cards have no identity on record, and then only the account counts: cards saved before
5 November 2026, cards Adyen returned no alias for, and delegated payment tokens. Saving the card
again fixes the first two.

On an event with a limit, a purchase Alyte cannot tie to a buyer is refused. An example is an
organisation's agent with no individual buyer behind it. The limit never lets an unidentified
purchase through.

Recognising the same person by email, your own customer ID or address is planned. Until then,
someone with two buyer accounts and a different card on each counts as two customers.

### Adyen: send the card alias

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on alias info">

  </Step>
  <Step title="Save a card to check">

## What counts

- Tickets held but not yet paid for, tickets bought, and tickets whose payment outcome is still
  being confirmed.
- A hold that is released or expires gives its tickets back to the customer's allowance.
- Add-ons such as parking or merchandise do not count, unless the add-on tier is set to count
  toward the party size.

The check runs in the same step that reserves the stock. Two agents working for one customer at
the same moment cannot both get in under the limit.

**The card is checked at payment.** Which card pays is certain only when the purchase is confirmed,
so the card's allowance is claimed then, before anything is sent to your payment provider, for a
single hold and for every line of a basket.

- Over the limit: `409 purchase_limit_exceeded`, and the hold is released. No payment is started.
- A declined payment gives the card's tickets back. A payment whose outcome is uncertain keeps them
  counted until it is confirmed.
- The cap on unpaid tickets (`maxHeldPerCustomer`) does not apply to the card, because these
  tickets are being paid for.

## Sales made before you set the limit

The first time you set a limit on an event, Alyte counts the event's tickets already sold. Each is
counted against the buyer who bought it, wherever Alyte's records confirm that buyer, and against
the card it was paid with, wherever that card has an identity on record.

- A customer who already has more than the limit keeps every ticket but cannot buy more. Nothing is
  cancelled.
- Changing the limit later does not count again. Removing it and setting it again does.
- Tickets that were held but unpaid at that moment are not counted.

`readiness` tells you how much of the event the limit covers. `ticketsCounted` is the held and
sold tickets it counts against a customer. `ticketsNotCounted` is the ones it cannot tie to one,
for example tickets bought before Alyte could identify the buyer.

## What agents and buyers see

**Discovery.** A limited event carries `purchaseLimit: { maxPerCustomer, maxHeldPerCustomer? }`, in
the agent's inventory view and in your public shop window, so an agent can plan within it.

**Refusals.** Nothing is held or charged when one of these is returned:

| Code | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| `409 purchase_limit_exceeded` (`details.limit`) | This purchase would take the customer over the event's limit | Tell the buyer the event's limit. A smaller quantity may still fit. |
| `409 held_limit_exceeded` (`details.limit`) | The customer already holds as many unpaid tickets for the event as you allow at once | Pay for or release an existing hold, then try again. |
| `409 purchase_limit_identity_required` | The event has a limit, and this purchase has no buyer Alyte can recognise | Buy through an agent the buyer created for themselves. |

## Queued purchases

A waiting [watch](/guides/lifecycle) counts toward the limit, unless you set
`countWaitingWatches: false`. Its tickets are counted under the buyer's account, and the card it
will pay with, from the moment it is queued.

- **Queueing over the limit is refused** with `409 purchase_limit_exceeded`, and no watch is created.
  Retrying a queue request with the same `requestId` never counts twice.
- **When the watch fires**, its hold takes over the counted tickets, so nothing counts twice. A
  watch that is cancelled, expires, fails or buys gives its tickets back at once.
- **A watch held back only by the buyer's own unpaid tickets keeps waiting**, with
  `waitingReason: "purchase_limit"` and no place in the line, and tries again when those tickets are
  paid for or released. Any other limit refusal fails the watch: you receive
  [`watch.failed`](/guides/webhooks) with the code as its `reason`, so you can tell the buyer.
- **Switching counting off** releases the event's waiting tickets at once.
- **Watches queued before** you set the limit, or switched counting on, are counted within about
  five minutes, even past the limit. Each is checked again when it fires.

Leave counting on unless you want a buyer to be able to queue for more than they could buy.

## Availability

Live on sandbox since revision `alyte-sandbox-00136-h2z`; recognising cards and counting queued
purchases since `alyte-sandbox-00137-x9w`. A deployment where ticket limits are not enabled refuses
`PUT` with `503 purchase_limits_unavailable` and changes nothing.
