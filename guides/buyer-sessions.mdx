---
title: "Bring your own auth"
description: "Your users, your login, your database — Alyte only needs an attestation."
---

Your customers already have accounts with you. Alyte doesn't replace your auth
or your user database — it plugs into them.

## How it works

1. A user is signed in **on your site**, with your auth.
2. They want an AI agent to buy tickets for them.
3. Your **server** attests them to Alyte (API token, `merchant:write`):

```ts
const s = await alyte.buyerSessions.mint({
  merchantId: 'mrc_…',
  email: user.email,
});
// → { buyerId: 'byr_…', redeemPath: '/buyer/redeem?token=…', expiresAt }
```

Optional fields open the page as a checkout for one ticket type: `tierId`,
`quantity` (1–10), `returnUrl` (https, under your registered embed origin) and
`lang` (a language tag such as `de` or `pt-BR`; the page also honours the
buyer's own choice and browser language).

4. You redirect the user to `ALYTE_URL + s.redeemPath`.
5. They arrive on the shop's authorize page already signed in, and grant their
   agent a mandate: spending cap, validity window, optional event scope.

## Your attestation is trusted for buying, not for granting

A session you mint is **merchant-attested**: Alyte believes your server that
this is `user.email`, but nothing has yet shown that the person in the browser
controls that inbox. So the session is **bounded**:

- It **can** act inside authority the buyer already granted: view their agents,
  mandates and purchases, and let an agent buy within an existing mandate.
- It **cannot create or widen** spending authority on your word alone. Saving a
  card, creating an agent, granting a mandate, or changing an agent's card first
  requires the buyer to prove they own the inbox.

On the authorize page this is one extra step, handled entirely by Alyte: when the
buyer first tries to grant, the page shows **Verify your email**, sends a
6-digit code to the attested address, and continues once they enter it. The
verified credential lasts 30 minutes; a code allows five attempts. Your server
never sees the code or the resulting credential, and there is nothing to change
in your integration.

What it means for you:

- **Test with inboxes you can read.** In sandbox the code is a real email.
  Automated browser tests that drive the authorize page must read that inbox, or
  stop before the grant step.
- **A direct call** to one of those buyer endpoints with a bounded session is
  refused with `403 step_up_required`. That is the page's cue to ask for the
  code, not an error to retry.

This applies to every merchant, so a mistaken or compromised attestation can
never hand an agent new spending power.

## Properties worth knowing

- **Single-use, short-lived.** The redeem link works once and expires in 15
  minutes. Hashed at rest, rate-limited per email.
- **Stable identity.** `buyerId` is deterministic per email — a user who arrives
  via your attestation today and via the email magic-link fallback tomorrow is
  the *same* buyer, with the same agents and mandates.
- **Tenant-locked.** Your API token can only mint sessions into your own shops.
- **The mandate is enforced by Alyte, not the agent.** A prompt-injected or
  malfunctioning agent cannot exceed the cap or scope the buyer granted —
  every check runs server-side.

## Fallback: magic link

No integration? Buyers can always use the shop page's built-in email magic
link. The attestation flow above is the same machinery with the email step
replaced by your session — which is why the identities converge.
